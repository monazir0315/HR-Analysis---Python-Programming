# Niche brief

Step 1 of `audience-analysis-steps.md`. Every agent reads this file first.

## Product

A lightweight web app for recruiters that screens applicants against the role's must-have criteria, verifies the claims of shortlisted candidates before interview, drafts specific, job-related feedback for unsuccessful candidates, and gives HR a dashboard of insights across roles and applications. It works on its own or alongside an existing ATS through CSV import. Every consequential decision stays with a human.

## Who is who

| Role | Who | Cares about |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Head of HR / People, Talent Acquisition lead, owner in a small company | cost per hire, time to hire, legal risk, reputation |
| Daily user | recruiters, HR generalists | less manual checking, no chasing documents, less admin |
| Affected party | candidates | fairness, minimal data requests, real feedback |
| Consumer of the output | hiring managers | interview-ready candidates, a defensible trail |

## Candidate niches (who × job)

| # | Who | Job to be done |
|---|---|---|
| A | small and mid-sized employers (about 50 to 500 staff), small HR team, high application volume | "Get me to a trustworthy shortlist without me checking every claim myself." |
| B | recruitment and staffing agencies | "Stop me finding out after submission or at offer stage that something was false." |
| C | regulated or credential-heavy employers (health, education, finance, public sector, engineering) | "Help me show a regulator or a candidate that the process was fair." |
| D | tech hiring teams | "Check portfolio and GitHub claims at scale." |
| A′ | SMEs, feedback job | "Let me reject people politely and specifically without spending an hour per email." |

Geography for the first estimate: Spain. Price model: open (per seat, per hire or per verified candidate); decided in step 13.

## The decision

Which niche the MVP's story, demo and fixtures are built around.

**By:** session 10.
**Current guess:** A or B (clearest pain, shortest sales cycle, easiest to interview). C is the best long-term story but the hardest to prove in two weeks.

## What would stop us

- Interviews show that verification is already handled by a background-check provider in every segment, and nobody feels the late-discovery pain.
- Candidates say they would drop out if asked for documents, even after shortlisting.
- Recruiters refuse another tool, and there is no realistic integration path (CSV or an ATS stub).
- The routes check fails for every niche even after the evidence is replaced.

## Evidence standard

An interview counts as evidence of demand only if it gives a specific recent incident, the time or money it cost, and a workaround already in use or paid for. Polite interest is not evidence.
